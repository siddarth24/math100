The big idea of this entire course

All of differential calculus comes down to one simple picture: if you zoom in close enough on a smooth curve, it looks like a straight line.

  • Chapters 2–3 (Limits & Derivatives): How to zoom in on a curve at a single point and measure its steepness (the derivative, or instantaneous rate of change).
  • Chapters 4–6 (Shortcut Rules & Rates): Fast rules for finding derivatives of any formula, plus how linked quantities move together in real time (related rates).
  • Chapters 7–9 (Using Derivatives): Using slopes to sketch curves, find the best possible outcome (optimization), and replace messy functions with simple polynomial twins (Taylor polynomials).
  • Chapters 10–16 (Your Flavour Topics): Using rates of change to model populations and differential equations (Flavours A & B) or optimizing across 3D surfaces with two inputs (Flavour C).

Which course and flavour are you in?

MATH 100 and MATH 180 take the exact same final exam. The only difference is that MATH 180 is designed for students who did not take calculus in high school, so it builds in extra precalculus review along the way.

About 70% of the course (Chapters 2 through 9.5) is identical for everyone. Your section's "flavour" only decides the application topics at the end of the term:

  • Flavour A: Physical sciences and engineering (adds Taylor error bounds, Newton's method, differential equations, and slope fields).
  • Flavour B: Life sciences (adds biological asymptotes, Newton's method, differential equations, slope fields, and biological phase-line models).
  • Flavour C: Commerce and social sciences (adds 3D surfaces, partial derivatives, and multivariable optimization with Lagrange multipliers).

Pick your Course and Flavour in the bar at the top of the page. The sidebar and checklist will automatically hide any topics your section does not cover, and choosing MATH 180 will automatically open each topic's precalculus warm-up box.

How the final exam works (and how to prioritize)

The final exam has 14 questions split into two equal halves:

  • Questions 1–9 (first half — 50% of your exam grade): Short, straightforward calculation problems shared by every section of MATH 100 and MATH 180, just like your weekly WeBWorK homework.
  • Questions 10–14 (harder half — 50% of your exam grade): Longer written word problems and explanations. Question 10 and Question 14 are shared by everyone; Questions 11–13 test your specific flavour (A, B, or C).

Short on study time? Master every problem tagged first half before touching harder half. Locking in the 9 routine WeBWorK-style questions first is the fastest, most reliable way to pass or bounce back from a rough midterm.

How to work through a topic page

  • 1. Read the intuition box at the top of the topic. It explains the core mental picture in plain English and translates the exact code-words UBC exams use (like "horizontal tangent" or "steady state") into concrete steps.
  • 2. Read the problem in the book's own words first. On the midterm and final, nobody translates the question for you. Try to spot what the question wants on your own, and click the red What this is asking line only when the wording has you stuck.
