Ch 5 — Related rates

Find how fast one measurement is changing when it is linked to another moving measurement (like a growing balloon's radius and volume) by differentiating a geometry formula with respect to time ttt.

The intuition: a moving movie vs. a single freeze-frame

Picture a balloon inflating or a ladder sliding down a wall: two or more measurements (like radius and volume) are changing at the same time, and they are locked together by a geometry formula. The golden rule: write the geometry equation that holds while everything is moving, differentiate with respect to time ttt first, and only plug in the "at the instant when..." snapshot numbers after derivatives appear. If you plug in a changing number too early, its derivative turns into 000 and kills the problem.

  • "How fast / at what rate is ___ changing?" → this tells you which rate d(something)dt\dfrac{\mathrm{d}(\text{something})}{\mathrm{d}t}dtd(something)​ is your goal.
  • "At the instant when the radius is 555..." → save this snapshot number for the very end, after you differentiate with respect to ttt.
  • "Shrinking / leaking / getting closer at rate 333" → if a distance or amount is getting smaller, write its rate with a minus sign (−3-3−3).
  • "Include units" → just write (units of the quantity) divided by (units of time), like cm3/s\text{cm}^3/\text{s}cm3/s for volume or rad/min\text{rad/min}rad/min for an angle.
