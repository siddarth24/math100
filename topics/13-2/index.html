13.2 — Other biological and chemical models

Read multi-species biological and chemical equations term by term, and use a fixed total (conservation law) to turn two linked differential equations into one.

The intuition: phase lines and push-pull balance in biology

In biological models like dydt=f(y)\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t} = f(y)dtdy​=f(y), the growth rate depends only on how big the population or concentration yyy is right now. If you graph the rate dydt\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}dtdy​ (vertical axis) against yyy (horizontal axis), you can read the entire future of the system at a glance: wherever the curve sits above the horizontal axis, dydt>0\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t} > 0dtdy​>0 so yyy grows (draw arrows pointing right →\to→); wherever it sits below the axis, dydt<0\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t} < 0dtdy​<0 so yyy shrinks (draw arrows pointing left ←\leftarrow←).

  • "Stable vs. unstable steady state" → steady states are the yyy-intercepts where dydt=0\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t} = 0dtdy​=0. If the arrows on both sides point inward toward the dot (→∙←\to \bullet \leftarrow→∙←), it is stable (a magnet); if they point away (←∘→\leftarrow \circ \to←∘→), it is unstable.
  • "What does each term in the equation mean biologically?" → a +++ term adds individuals or chemical (birth, inflow, production); a −-− term removes them (death, washout, breakdown). A product like x⋅yx \cdot yx⋅y means two things must bump into each other to react.
  • "Use a conservation law to eliminate a variable" → if total molecules y1+y2=Py_1 + y_2 = Py1​+y2​=P stay constant, replace y2y_2y2​ with P−y1P - y_1P−y1​ so two tangled equations simplify into one!
