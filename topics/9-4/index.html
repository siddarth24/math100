9.4 — Still higher derivatives — Taylor polynomials

Build degree-nnn Taylor polynomials Tn(x)=∑k=0nf(k)(a)k!(x−a)kT_n(x) = \sum_{k=0}^n \frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x - a)^kTn​(x)=∑k=0n​k!f(k)(a)​(x−a)k that match a function's first nnn derivatives at x=ax = ax=a, and read derivatives straight off a polynomial's coefficients.

The intuition: building a polynomial twin at a home-base point

Complicated functions like exe^xex, sin⁡x\sin xsinx, or ln⁡x\ln xlnx are hard to compute by hand, while polynomials are easy. A Taylor polynomial centered at an easy "home-base" point x=ax = ax=a builds a polynomial twin that matches the original function's height f(a)f(a)f(a), slope f′(a)f'(a)f′(a), bend f′′(a)f''(a)f′′(a), and higher derivatives right at x=ax = ax=a. A degree-111 Taylor polynomial is just the tangent line; adding a degree-222 term bends the line into a parabola that hugs the curve even longer.

  • "Linear (T1T_1T1​), quadratic (T2T_2T2​), or degree-nnn (TnT_nTn​) Taylor polynomial about x=ax = ax=a" → compute f(a),f′(a),…,f(n)(a)f(a), f'(a), \dots, f^{(n)}(a)f(a),f′(a),…,f(n)(a) at the anchor aaa and plug into Tn(x)=∑k=0nf(k)(a)k!(x−a)kT_n(x) = \sum_{k=0}^n \dfrac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x - a)^kTn​(x)=∑k=0n​k!f(k)(a)​(x−a)k. ("Maclaurin" just means the home base is a=0a = 0a=0.)
  • "Given a Taylor polynomial, find f(k)(a)f^{(k)}(a)f(k)(a)" → do not differentiate the whole polynomial! Just look at the number sitting in front of (x−a)k(x - a)^k(x−a)k: that coefficient equals f(k)(a)k!\dfrac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}k!f(k)(a)​, so multiply it by k!k!k!.
  • "Estimate a number (like 102\sqrt{102}102​ or e0.1e^{0.1}e0.1) without a calculator" → pick the nearest clean home base aaa you know by heart (like a=100a = 100a=100 for x\sqrt{x}x​ or a=0a = 0a=0 for exe^xex), build the Taylor polynomial at aaa, and plug in the target xxx.
  • "Bound the error ∣Rn(x)∣|R_n(x)|∣Rn​(x)∣" (Flavour A) → use the next term in the pattern, ∣f(n+1)(c)(n+1)!(x−a)n+1∣\left|\dfrac{f^{(n+1)}(c)}{(n+1)!}(x - a)^{n+1}\right|​(n+1)!f(n+1)(c)​(x−a)n+1​, and replace ∣f(n+1)(c)∣|f^{(n+1)}(c)|∣f(n+1)(c)∣ with the largest value it can possibly reach between aaa and xxx.
