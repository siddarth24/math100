Ch 10 — Newton’s method

Zero in on solutions to f(x)=0f(x) = 0f(x)=0 that algebra cannot solve by repeatedly sliding down the tangent line: xn+1=xn−f(xn)f′(xn)x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}xn+1​=xn​−f′(xn​)f(xn​)​.

The intuition: sliding down the tangent line to find where $f(x) = 0$

Suppose you want to solve an equation that algebra cannot crack, by finding where a graph crosses the xxx-axis (f(x)=0f(x) = 0f(x)=0). Newton's method starts at a reasonable guess x0x_0x0​, draws the tangent line to the curve at x0x_0x0​, and rides that tangent line straight to where it hits the xxx-axis. That landing spot becomes your much better next guess: xn+1=xn−f(xn)f′(xn)x_{n+1} = x_n - \dfrac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}xn+1​=xn​−f′(xn​)f(xn​)​.

  • "Find x1x_1x1​ (or x2x_2x2​) starting from x0x_0x0​" → plug x0x_0x0​ into x1=x0−f(x0)f′(x0)x_1 = x_0 - \dfrac{f(x_0)}{f'(x_0)}x1​=x0​−f′(x0​)f(x0​)​. If asked for x2x_2x2​, repeat the exact same formula using x1x_1x1​.
  • "Solve g(x)=h(x)g(x) = h(x)g(x)=h(x) or approximate 73\sqrt[3]{7}37​" → Newton's method only finds zeros! First move everything to one side so your function equals zero: f(x)=g(x)−h(x)=0f(x) = g(x) - h(x) = 0f(x)=g(x)−h(x)=0 (or f(x)=x3−7=0f(x) = x^3 - 7 = 0f(x)=x3−7=0).
  • "Approximate a critical point of g(x)g(x)g(x)" → a critical point is where g′(x)=0g'(x) = 0g′(x)=0, so apply Newton's method to the derivative function f(x)=g′(x)f(x) = g'(x)f(x)=g′(x) (which uses g′′(x)g''(x)g′′(x) in the bottom of the formula!).
  • "Why did Newton's method fail?" → if f′(xn)=0f'(x_n) = 0f′(xn​)=0, the tangent line is flat and never hits the xxx-axis; if f′(xn)f'(x_n)f′(xn​) is tiny, the tangent line shoots your next guess miles away.
