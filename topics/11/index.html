Ch 11 — Introduction to differential equations

Understand differential equations—equations that describe how fast a quantity grows or decays—check candidate solutions, and find where a system settles down (steady states).

The intuition: a rule for how fast something grows or shrinks

In normal algebra, you solve for a mystery number xxx. In a differential equation, you are given a rule connecting a quantity y(t)y(t)y(t) to its own speed dydt\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}dtdy​ (for example, "a population grows at a rate proportional to its current size"), and your goal is to find the formula y(t)y(t)y(t) that obeys that rule. Good news: half the time you do not even have to solve from scratch—if the question hands you a formula and asks you to verify it, just take its derivative and show both sides of the equation match!

  • "Verify / show that y(t)=…y(t) = \dotsy(t)=… is a solution" → differentiate the given y(t)y(t)y(t) formula to get dydt\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}dtdy​, plug both into the left and right sides of the differential equation, and simplify until both sides match.
  • "Initial condition y(0)=y0y(0) = y_0y(0)=y0​" → plug t=0t = 0t=0 and y=y0y = y_0y=y0​ into your general solution at the end to pin down the mystery constant CCC.
  • "Rate is proportional to ___" → translate "rate of change of yyy" as dydt\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}dtdy​ and "proportional to AAA" as k⋅Ak \cdot Ak⋅A.
  • "Steady state / equilibrium" → a value of yyy where nothing changes anymore (dydt=0\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t} = 0dtdy​=0). Set the rate formula equal to 000 and solve for yyy.
Foundation
Before this (precalculus & setup)

Remember: the solution to a differential equation is a whole function y(t)y(t)y(t), not a single number. And a "steady state" just means the flat value of yyy where dydt=0\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t} = 0dtdy​=0.
