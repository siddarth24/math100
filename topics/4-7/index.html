4.7 — Inverse trigonometric functions and their derivatives

Differentiate inverse trig functions (arcsin⁡,arccos⁡,arctan⁡,arcsec⁡\arcsin, \arccos, \arctan, \operatorname{arcsec}arcsin,arccos,arctan,arcsec) and find the slope of an inverse function f−1f^{-1}f−1 by flipping the slope of fff.

The intuition: spotting the outer shape before you start

Shortcut rules let you find slopes without doing a limit every time. Before you write anything down, zoom out and ask how the formula is glued together: are two things multiplied (product rule), divided (quotient rule), or stuffed one inside another like Russian dolls (chain rule)? When you differentiate nested layers, peel from the outside in—differentiate the outer wrapper first while leaving the inside untouched, then multiply by the derivative of the inside.

  • "Equation of the tangent line at x=ax = ax=a" → you only need two numbers: the height y0=f(a)y_0 = f(a)y0​=f(a) and the slope m=f′(a)m = f'(a)m=f′(a). Drop them straight into y=y0+m(x−a)y = y_0 + m(x - a)y=y0​+m(x−a).
  • "Where is the tangent line horizontal?" → flat lines have slope 000, so set f′(x)=0f'(x) = 0f′(x)=0 and solve for xxx.
  • "Variable in both the base and the power (like xxx^xxx)" → take ln⁡\lnln of both sides first (or rewrite as epower⋅ln⁡(base)e^{\text{power} \cdot \ln(\text{base})}epower⋅ln(base)) so the messy exponent drops down in front.
  • "xxx and yyy mixed together in an equation (implicit)" → differentiate both sides with respect to xxx, tacking on a dydx\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}dxdy​ (chain rule!) every time you differentiate a yyy term, then solve for dydx\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}dxdy​.
