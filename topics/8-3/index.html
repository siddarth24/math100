8.3 — Max/min examples

Turn real-world "find the best/cheapest/largest" word problems into a one-variable calculus problem and find the winning value.

The intuition: finding the highest peak or lowest valley

The highest and lowest points on a graph can only hide in three places: 1. flat peaks or valleys where the slope is zero (f′(x)=0f'(x) = 0f′(x)=0, called critical points), 2. sharp corners where the slope is undefined (singular points), or 3. the very ends of the allowed interval (endpoints). In word problems, you always have two sentences to hunt for: the goal (what you want to make as big or small as possible) and the constraint (the fixed budget, fencing, or volume that lets you get rid of extra variables).

  • "Critical point vs. singular point" → both must be inside the domain of fff: a critical point has a flat tangent (f′(c)=0f'(c) = 0f′(c)=0); a singular point has a broken derivative (f′(c)f'(c)f′(c) does not exist, like a sharp corner).
  • "Global (absolute) max/min on a closed interval [a,b][a, b][a,b]" → make a simple table, plug your critical points, singular points, and both endpoints aaa and bbb into the original f(x)f(x)f(x), and pick the biggest and smallest outputs.
  • "Maximize area / minimize cost / closest point" → write a formula for that target quantity first, then use the given fixed number (the constraint) to substitute out a second variable so only one variable remains.
